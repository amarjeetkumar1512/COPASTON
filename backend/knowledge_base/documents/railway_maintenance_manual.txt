Document Type: Product Lifecycle, Safety and O&M Knowledge Base
Version: 1.0
System: COPASTON
Equipment: Railway Maintenance and Inspection Vehicle


1. PURPOSE

This document provides basic maintenance, inspection and safety guidance
for railway maintenance and inspection equipment managed through COPASTON.

COPASTON uses this knowledge to support maintenance teams, safety teams and
operators in identifying maintenance requirements, safety risks and
recommended actions.


2. PREVENTIVE MAINTENANCE

Preventive maintenance should be performed according to the equipment
maintenance schedule.

The main preventive maintenance activities include:

- Visual inspection of the complete machine.
- Inspection of brake system components.
- Inspection of hydraulic hoses and fittings.
- Inspection of engine oil and coolant levels.
- Inspection of electrical connections.
- Inspection of battery condition.
- Inspection of tyres, wheels and mechanical fasteners.
- Inspection of safety guards and emergency stop devices.
- Cleaning of important mechanical and electrical components.

Any abnormal condition found during inspection should be recorded in the
COPASTON maintenance system.


3. BRAKE SYSTEM MAINTENANCE

The brake system is a critical safety system.

Brake inspection should include:

- Checking brake response.
- Checking brake hoses and connections.
- Checking for hydraulic or pneumatic leakage.
- Checking brake pads or brake components for excessive wear.
- Checking brake fluid or brake system pressure according to equipment
  requirements.

If brake response is delayed, weak or abnormal, the equipment should not
be operated until the problem has been investigated.

A brake failure should be recorded as a high-priority safety incident.


4. HYDRAULIC SYSTEM

The hydraulic system should be inspected regularly.

Maintenance personnel should check:

- Hydraulic oil level.
- Hydraulic hoses.
- Hydraulic fittings.
- Hydraulic cylinders.
- Signs of oil leakage.
- Abnormal noise during hydraulic operation.

Hydraulic leakage should be treated as a safety and maintenance concern.

Damaged hydraulic hoses should be replaced before the equipment is returned
to normal operation.


5. ENGINE AND POWER SYSTEM

Engine inspection should include:

- Engine oil level.
- Coolant level.
- Fuel system condition.
- Unusual engine noise.
- Leakage around the engine.
- Battery and starting system condition.

Low engine oil or coolant levels should be corrected before operation.

Unusual engine noise or excessive leakage requires further inspection.


6. ELECTRICAL SYSTEM

Electrical inspection should include:

- Battery terminals.
- Electrical wiring.
- Connectors.
- Fuses.
- Control switches.
- Warning indicators.
- Emergency stop system.

Loose or damaged electrical connections should be repaired before operation.

Electrical faults that affect machine control or safety systems should be
treated as high-priority issues.


7. SAFETY INSPECTION

Before operating railway maintenance equipment, operators should perform
a basic safety inspection.

The inspection should verify:

- Braking system is functioning.
- Emergency stop system is functioning.
- Warning indicators are working.
- No visible hydraulic leakage is present.
- No major mechanical damage is visible.
- Safety guards are installed correctly.
- Required maintenance is not overdue.

The equipment should not be operated when a critical safety defect is
identified.


8. SAFETY INCIDENT SEVERITY

Safety incidents can be classified using the following levels:

LOW:
Minor issue with limited operational impact.

MEDIUM:
Issue that may affect equipment performance and requires maintenance
attention.

HIGH:
Issue that can create significant safety risk or equipment failure.

CRITICAL:
Condition that creates an immediate and serious safety risk. Equipment
operation should be stopped until the condition is resolved.


9. MAINTENANCE PRIORITY

Maintenance priority should be based on safety impact and equipment
condition.

High-priority maintenance should be assigned when:

- Brake performance is abnormal.
- Critical hydraulic leakage is detected.
- Emergency stop system fails.
- Major electrical safety fault is detected.
- A critical mechanical component is damaged.

Routine inspection findings can be scheduled as preventive maintenance.


10. MAINTENANCE RECORD

Every maintenance activity should contain:

- Product ID.
- Maintenance type.
- Description of work.
- Maintenance due date.
- Current maintenance status.

Typical maintenance statuses are:

PENDING:
Maintenance is scheduled but not completed.

IN PROGRESS:
Maintenance activity is currently being performed.

COMPLETED:
Maintenance activity has been completed successfully.

OVERDUE:
Scheduled maintenance has passed its due date.


11. SAFETY INCIDENT RECORD

A safety incident should contain:

- Product ID.
- Incident type.
- Severity.
- Description.
- Incident status.

Typical incident statuses are:

OPEN:
Incident requires investigation or action.

IN PROGRESS:
Corrective action is being performed.

RESOLVED:
Corrective action has been completed.


12. CORRECTIVE ACTION

When a safety-critical problem is detected:

1. Stop operation if continued operation may create additional risk.
2. Record the safety incident.
3. Identify the affected product.
4. Inspect the affected system.
5. Perform corrective maintenance.
6. Verify that the problem has been resolved.
7. Update the maintenance and safety records.
8. Return the equipment to operation only after the required checks
   are completed.
